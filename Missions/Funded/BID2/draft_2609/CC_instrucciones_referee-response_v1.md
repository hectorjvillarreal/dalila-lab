## 2. EDIT A — "Fully estimated" and "fully disciplined" (four locations)

The referee's objection is well founded and technical: two internally estimated
parameters lie at their bounds, one preference parameter is pinned because the
objective is flat in that dimension, standard errors are not reported, and the
local rank condition is not established at the current estimates. "Fully
estimated" claims more than the method delivers.

**A1 — Abstract, line ~103.** Replace

> The model is fully estimated by classical minimum distance on ten moments, nine
> of them from Mexican micro-data, including earnings profiles from household
> expenditure surveys.

with

> The model is calibrated and estimated in two stages, with nine structural
> parameters disciplined by minimum distance using ten moments, nine of them
> estimated from Mexican micro-data.

**A2 — Section 2, line ~129.** The phrase "a negative result that only a fully
disciplined calibration could have produced". Replace "fully disciplined
calibration" with a formulation that does not overclaim — for example "a
calibration disciplined on the full moment set". Keep the sentence's argument
intact: the point that an illustrative parameterization would never have been
forced to confront the gradient is correct and stays.

**A3 — Section 2, line ~170.** "a fully disciplined calibration of the investment
block to Mexican micro-data confronts, moment by moment…". Same replacement.

**A4 — Section 7, line ~1326.** "meets a boundary that only a fully disciplined
estimation exposes". Same replacement.

Use a consistent formulation across A2–A4. Report the phrase chosen.

## 3. EDIT B — "Cannot identify" (abstract, line ~103)

The abstract currently ends:

> the two are different objects, and moments generated by the second cannot
> identify the first.

This is more categorical than the evidence supports. What the paper establishes
is bounded by the specification and the target set. Replace the final clause so
that it reads, in substance:

> the two are different objects, and moments generated by the second are poorly
> suited to identifying the first in this specification.

Preserve everything earlier in the sentence. The qualification "in this
specification" is the operative addition and must appear.

**Check Section 5 and Section 7 for the same categorical construction.** Search
for "cannot identify", "cannot be identified", and "cannot discipline". Where the
claim is made without a specification-bounded qualifier, add one. Report every
instance found and what was done. Where the surrounding text already carries the
qualifier — several passages in Section 5 do — change nothing.

## 4. EDIT C — "A measurement, not a defect" (two locations)

The referee reads this construction as defensive, and on re-reading it is: it
asserts the conclusion the reader should reach independently. The substance is
right; the framing invites the doubt it denies.

**C1 — Section 5, line ~1105.** The paragraph opens "The result is a measurement,
not a defect. Nothing in it says the Grossman-descended investment block is wrong
as theory, and nothing in it says the implementation failed…". Recast the opening
so that it states the interpretation positively rather than by denial — for
example, that the pattern is evidence of a mismatch between the economic object
represented in the model and the object recorded in the expenditure data. **Keep
the remainder of the paragraph**, including the point that a partially calibrated
model would have hidden the tension.

**C2 — Section 1, line ~127.** "The diagnosis is a mismatch of objects, not a
defect of implementation." Delete the ", not a defect of implementation" clause;
the positive half carries the claim.

**Do not** apply this to Section 7 line ~1334, where "not a defect that qualifies
the fiscal findings" is doing different work — it demarcates the scope of the
health result against the fiscal results, which is a substantive distinction the
referee endorsed. Leave it.

## 5. EDIT D — "Aggregate welfare event" (three locations)

The referee is right that this conflates newborn welfare with aggregate welfare.
The model shows newborn welfare barely changes while same-age comparisons across
stationary equilibria generate substantial losses rising with age. The
reformulation is more accurate **and stronger**.

Apply at three locations, adapting to each context:

- **abstract, line ~103**: "aging under unchanged policy redistributes across
  cohorts rather than lowering aggregate welfare"
- **Section 1, line ~123**: "Aging under unchanged policy is a redistribution
  across cohorts, not an aggregate welfare event."
- **Section 7, line ~1328**: "demographic aging under unchanged policy is a
  redistribution across cohorts rather than an aggregate welfare event"

The licensed formulation is, in substance: *in the stationary comparison, aging
generates little change in newborn welfare but substantial redistribution in
remaining-lifetime welfare across ages and asset positions.*

Adapt the wording to each sentence rather than pasting one form three times. The
two required elements everywhere: **newborn** welfare (not aggregate) is what is
nearly unchanged, and what redistributes is **remaining-lifetime** welfare across
ages and asset positions.

## 6. EDIT E — The contribution hierarchy (two additions)

The referee's principal comment is that the paper contains two contributions with
no stated hierarchy. The document's **ordering already implements** the intended
hierarchy — fiscal results first, health measurement result second, in both the
introduction and the conclusion. What is missing is that the hierarchy is never
**stated**. This is the highest-return edit in the pass and it is two sentences.

**E1 — Section 1.** At the end of the paragraph at line ~121 (the paragraph
describing what the model is and how it is disciplined), or at the start of the
paragraph at line ~123, add two sentences naming the hierarchy explicitly, in
substance:

> The paper's principal economic contribution is the general-equilibrium
> incidence of demographic aging and pension adjustment. Its methodological
> contribution is the measurement result that follows when a health-investment
> block is taken seriously to Mexican micro-data.

Do not use these words verbatim; write them in the register of the surrounding
prose. Report the location chosen and the sentences written.

**E2 — Section 7.** Add the corresponding statement to the conclusion, placed so
that it introduces rather than repeats the results that follow. One or two
sentences. It must be recognizably the same hierarchy, not a restatement in
different terms.

**Nothing is demoted.** The health block remains the methodological contribution
and Section 5 keeps its place and length. This edit names the ordering the
document already has.

## 7. EDIT F — The gender qualification (title block and Section 1)

The referee notes the title promises more gender content than the model contains:
sex enters through earnings profiles, survival schedules, and population weights,
with no bargaining, participation margin, or care allocation.

**Do not change the title.** Instead, add an early clarifying clause in Section 1
— at the first point where sex heterogeneity is introduced — stating that
sex-differentiated results here arise from measured earnings and survival
profiles and population composition, and that the model contains no household
bargaining or intra-household allocation. One sentence.

Section 2's gender subsection already makes this point; the introduction does
not, and the introduction is where a reader forms the expectation.
