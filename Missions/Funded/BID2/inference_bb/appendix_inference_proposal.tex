The survey targets and their sampling distribution are obtained by a single procedure, a Bayesian bootstrap clustered at the primary sampling unit \citep{Rubin1981, AndrewsMoonShapiro2026}: each sampling unit receives an exponential weight in every replicate, the expansion factors are rescaled accordingly, and all nine survey moments are recomputed jointly on the reweighted sample. The intervals reported in Table~\ref{tab:targeted_moments} are the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of 2,000 replicates and have a credible-interval interpretation; the replicate standard deviations coincide with the cluster-robust standard errors to within five percent for the ENIGH moments. We nevertheless weight proportionally rather than by inverse variance, because inverse-variance weighting turns the estimator into a matcher of $t$-statistics rather than of economic magnitudes: at the starting values it concentrated the objective on a single precisely estimated survey moment, leaving the moments that carry the paper's mechanism nearly weightless.

\paragraph{Inference.} Table~\ref{tab:moment_match_md} reports, next to each data moment, its 95 percent credible interval from the Bayesian bootstrap. Five of the seven ENIGH moments generated by the model at the estimates lie outside the interval of their empirical counterpart, and a joint Mahalanobis check of the seven model moments against the replicate distribution rejects at any conventional level. The discrepancies in Table~\ref{tab:moment_match_md} are therefore misspecification rather than sampling noise; Section~\ref{subsec:health_block_evidence} and Appendix~\ref{subsec:health_evidence} discuss their source in the health block. For the parameters themselves we report point estimates without standard errors. The numerical derivatives that the minimum-distance sandwich formula requires are not stable here, and two estimates sit at boundaries of the admissible set, where the unconstrained sandwich does not apply. The natural alternative, re-estimating the parameters on each bootstrap replicate of the moment vector and reporting percentile intervals together with the share of replicates at a bound, requires no derivative and respects the admissible set; it is left for future work because of its computational cost.
