# BID2 Draft-November: Bayesian bootstrap of the calibration targets

2026-11-08. Report for Héctor and Diego. Code and outputs in `Missions/Funded/BID2/inference_bb/`; figure `figures/bb_targets.png`.
Method: Rubin (1981), as recommended by Andrews, Moon and Shapiro (2026, *JEP* 40(4)). One Exp(1) weight per primary sampling unit, multiplied into the expansion factor, every target recomputed jointly on each reweighted sample. ENIGH: pooled 2016 and 2018, 16,268 PSUs (wave × upm), 144,958 households, 2,000 replicates, seed 20261108. ENASEM: 2018→2021 wave pair, 2,243 PSUs, 4,000 replicates. Intervals are 2.5 and 97.5 percent quantiles of the replicate distribution; they read as credible intervals.

Reading the columns. *Reproduced* is the point estimate on Dalila under the definitions in `src/enigh_moments_bb.py` (`DEFINITIONS`): the three shares reproduce exactly, the elasticities and the log-slope to the second decimal, the skill premium does not (see §4). *BB SD* against *Draft SE*: the bootstrap standard deviation equals the draft's cluster-robust standard error for every ENIGH moment to within 5 percent, except the skill premium (0.016 vs 0.013). *p-p distance* is Andrews and Shapiro's (2026) measure of the gap between the bootstrap and the normal approximation with the draft's SE; values below about 0.06 mean the normal summary is adequate. *Model* is the model moment at the estimates (tab:moment_match_md); *Tail prob.* is the two-sided posterior probability of a data moment at least as far from the center as the model moment.

Joint check of the seven model moments against the replicate cloud: squared Mahalanobis distance 821 against a 95 percent replicate quantile of 14.6; posterior tail probability 0.0000. Replicate correlations worth knowing: the two elasticities move together (0.96), and the 65–79 share with the log-slope (0.48); the rest are below 0.3 in absolute value.

## 3. What this means for the paper

- **The draft's standard errors are right, and the normal summary is adequate for the ENIGH moments.** The bootstrap reproduces the cluster-robust SEs almost exactly and the p-p distances are small. The appendix's worry about incommensurable SEs (cluster-robust, delta method, clustered bootstrap) disappears under one procedure that produces all nine at once, with their joint covariance. The argument against inverse-variance weighting then rests only on its economic leg, which stands on its own.
- **The moment fit is not within sampling error, and the table should say so.** Five of the seven ENIGH model moments fall outside the 95 percent credible interval of the data moment; only the 50–64 share and the skill premium are inside. The joint distance is about fifty times the 95 percent replicate quantile. The sentence "the fit of Table tab:moment_match_md, rather than a t-statistic, is the measure of precision on offer" should become a statement that the misses are misspecification, not sampling noise. This is consistent with what the paper already argues about the health block (Section 4.5 and Appendix D): the data are precise enough to reject the preventive-spending mechanism's implications for the income elasticities and the old-age share.
- **For the survival moments the normal approximation is the wrong summary.** Survival near one is bounded, the posterior is skewed, and a symmetric interval from the delta method overstates the upper tail. Reporting quantile intervals costs nothing here.
- **Standard errors on the parameters remain the open item, and the Bayesian bootstrap is the route.** The sandwich fails for the two reasons the appendix gives (unstable numerical Jacobian, two boundary estimates). Re-estimating θ on R reweighted moment vectors sidesteps both: no derivative is needed, and a parameter at a bound is reported as the share of replicates that reach it, with quantile intervals that respect the admissible set. Specification in §5.

## 4. Caveats on the reproduction

- ENIGH 2016 was downloaded on 2026-11-08 from INEGI's direct microdata pattern (zip signatures verified); the draft's own extraction code was not available, so definitions were inferred from the Measurement paragraph and chosen to reproduce the point targets. They are recorded in `DEFINITIONS`.
- The skill premium is 0.691 under "upper secondary or more" (persons aged 25–34 with positive labor income, wave fixed effect) and 0.803 under "university or more"; neither equals 0.659. Men only gives 0.530; adding a sex dummy gives 0.740. The education split and the earnings definition (sum of `ing_tri` over the labor claves, which matches INEGI's household `ingtrab` within 1 percent for 78 percent of households) need Diego's confirmation before the skill-premium interval is quoted.
- Stratification (`est_dis`) is ignored, as the Bayesian bootstrap with cluster weights does; this is conservative (slightly wider intervals). The near-equality with the draft's SEs says the stratification gain is negligible here.
- The joint check treats the model moments as fixed numbers; it asks whether the data could plausibly have produced them, which is the question the fit table answers informally.

## 5. Specification for the parameter step (Diego's side)

1. Run the moment code once per replicate with the same cluster weights used here. Inputs: for each wave, a seed, the PSU identifier, and `w_r = factor × Exp(1)_PSU`. Output: a matrix of R rows × 9 survey moments (the hours target stays fixed). Suggested R: 200 for the moments, of which the first 25–50 are re-estimated.
2. For each selected replicate, re-estimate θ by the same minimum-distance criterion with the replicate's moment vector as target, warm-started at θ̂, in partial equilibrium as in the paper. Record whether each of Ξ and π̄ʰ hits its bound.
3. Report, per parameter: point estimate, 2.5 and 97.5 percent quantiles, and the share of replicates at a bound. Apply the split-half rule: if the two halves of the replicates give the same intervals to the precision printed, R is enough.
4. Report the moment-fit table with a "95 % credible interval" column from step 1 and the joint distance from §1. The draws from this folder (`outputs/enigh_moments_bb_draws.csv`) can serve as a check against step 1 for the seven ENIGH moments once definitions are aligned.
5. Cost: unknown for the current solver. For reference, Dalila's older six-moment version evaluates the partial-equilibrium objective in about 6 minutes, so a warm-started re-estimation of 30–60 evaluations is 3–6 hours per replicate; 25 replicates is a week of background compute on one machine, or a day on a small cluster. The moment step (1) costs seconds.
