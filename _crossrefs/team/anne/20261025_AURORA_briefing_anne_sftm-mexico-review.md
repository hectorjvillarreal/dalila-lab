# Anne — review of the SFTM México note, demographic lens

**Ask.** Review §3.4 "Demographic realism" of the note (the doc's "Demographic realism (Anne)"
section). Then endorse it or return it with corrections. Your endorsement fills `endorsed_by` for
§3.4. The transcription check on the EIC 2025 figures (0041c53) is a separate, earlier step. This
briefing is about the argument.

**The paper.** Fernández-Villaverde, "Saving for the Machines", first draft 2026-10-20. The
household is infinitely lived, with no life cycle, no aging and no dissaving cohort. Every México
statement in §3.4 is tagged [I], our inference.

## What to check

1. **The upper-bound reading.**
   - **The claim:** the paper's saving response is an upper bound, and DFD's OLG framework sits at
     the other end.
   - **The anchors:** the paper's own literature review ("saving comes out of wages, so automation
     that depresses wages chokes off investment"; Sachs & Kotlikoff 2012, Benzell et al. 2015). In
     its hand-to-mouth extension, the saving rate on impact falls from 30% to 21% (σ = 0.2,
     θ = 0.5).
   - **Question:** do you accept the framing?
2. **Bind, step 1: saving capacity peaks now.** The note dates the window from the total-dependency
   minimum (45.0 in 2030, within 1 point over 2027–2035). It then reads that window as the period
   when "prime-age savers are the largest share". The dependency minimum and the peak share of
   prime-age savers are not the same marker. Is the TDR window an acceptable proxy, or should the
   note use a savers-share measure?
3. **Bind, step 2: old age financed by transfers, not assets.**
   - **The claim:** aging in México raises transfer demand on the budget rather than asset demand
     that funds capital.
   - **The basis:** transition-generation PAYG liabilities and the constitutionally ranked
     non-contributory pension, citing the Auclert et al. note, gap 1.
   - **Scope:** the NTA treatment is kept available but deliberately not opened.
   - **Question:** is that the right boundary for this note?
4. **Bind, step 3: highest need, lowest capacity.** Labour scarcity raises the value of machines.
   It is also the force that turns México into an adopter in Benzell–Kotlikoff–Ye. Domestic saving
   falls on the same timetable. Is the "optimistic read versus financeability constraint" framing
   right?
5. **The BKY seam (Elle's comment 2, applied in 2694a21).** The note now says BKY's 2097 adoption
   date rests on WPP 2024 (TFR near 1.7 in 2050) and is "likely late" on EIC 2025. It cites BKY's
   own vintage switch (never adopts → 2097) as the direction check. Is "likely late" the right
   strength, or would you hold it at "direction known, size unknown"? Open question 8 is yours,
   with Nina.
