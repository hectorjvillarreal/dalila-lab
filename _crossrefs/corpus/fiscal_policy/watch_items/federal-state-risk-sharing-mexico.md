# Federal–state risk sharing in Mexico (reframed from "fiscal capacity vs reinsurance in a monetary union")

**Thread.** Chapter 4, §4.3.3.b (fiscal federalism, pp. 223–224), Box 4.19 (the US states' no-bailout regime, p. 222) and Box 4.20 (spillovers in a monetary union, p. 225) pose the stabilisation-versus-discipline problem for members of a currency area. Within a federation it is the same problem: Mexico's states share a currency, face a federal no-bailout norm of uncertain credibility, and are stabilised through transfers. FEIEF (Fondo de Estabilización de los Ingresos de las Entidades Federativas) is, in effect, a reinsurance mechanism.

**Why it matters.** Bears directly on the Ley de Disciplina Financiera de las Entidades Federativas y los Municipios (2016) and on RF's G2 (subnational finances) line.

**Open questions.** (1) How much of a state-level revenue shock does the federal budget absorb (Sachs–Sala-i-Martín-type estimate for Mexico)? (2) Does FEIEF's design produce acyclical state spending, as the Chile/Norway rules in Box 4.16 aim to? (3) Is the no-bailout norm credible after the 2016 law?

**Owner:** proposed G2 team; Cath supports on the budget-constraint side. Opened at Cath's request in the 2026-11-09 review (she declined the "multipliers at the zero lower bound" item as low relevance for Mexico; a possible replacement is *state-dependent multipliers in emerging markets* anchored on the IMF GFA country estimates).
