## Summary

Fernández-Villaverde's deck argues that the global TFR decline is faster than the
UN WPP records, with an acceleration around 2016 and 2023 as the likely first year
in human history that global fertility fell below replacement. Its load-bearing claim
for DFD is the third: middle- and low-income economies are transitioning faster and
deeper than advanced economies and overshooting them — the US (1.62) now sits above
Mexico (1.55), with Costa Rica (1.12), Colombia (1.06), and Chile (1.03) far lower.
The deck also documents systematic UN WPP over-projection: vital-registry births fall
below even the 2024 WPP medium variant (Colombia exhibit), and recent censuses force
downward level corrections (Brazil 203M vs 212M; Paraguay 6.1M vs 6.9M). It closes
with the fiscal/growth arithmetic of negative labour growth, Japanization as preview,
and the lifecycle incidence of the welfare state.

## DFD Calibration Implications

**Does this affect the fast-transition TFR scenario for Mexico?** Yes — it is the
generalizing anchor. It reframes LAC overshooting as a middle-income regularity rather
than a regional anomaly, which sustains the fast-transition path as the *central*
scenario and CELADE/WPP medium-variant as the *optimistic* one. The Colombia
births-vs-WPP gap is the single most useful exhibit for that case. No change to the
encoded central path (Mexico TFR = 1.5 stable) or stress path (TFR → 1.0 by 2030);
the deck strengthens the standing discipline rather than revising it.

**Does this affect the dependency-ratio path feeding IM-6's pension contribution rate?**
Indirectly but materially. The corrected replacement rate (~2.2–2.33 for emerging
markets, not 2.1, via lower survival-to-30 and the sex-ratio term) places LAC cases
further below replacement than the naive benchmark, deepening the long-run
old-age dependency trajectory. The rule of 85 (long-run population ≈ 85 × annual births)
is adopted as a first-order sanity rail on the fast-transition population path.
Route the gᵧ = g(y/l) + gₗ arithmetic and the lifecycle fiscal-incidence point to
Cath / the IM-6 fiscal block.

**Does this affect survival probabilities in the OLG demographic block?** Not directly.
The deck's mortality content is the aging coda (rule of 85, life-expectancy framing),
which bears on BDH and on long-run population accounting, not on near-term survival
calibration for the OLG core.

**Does this affect coupling/partnership formation as an upstream fertility driver?**
Yes — strongest cross-link for the Demographics workspace. The extensive-margin claim
(the sub-2.1 drop is people not forming childbearing unions, not smaller families) is
the macro-demographic dual of the Stage 1.5 union-composition finding, corroborating
union *composition* as the operative state variable from the top down. It does **not**,
however, discriminate between within-cohort cascade and cohort-replacement: F-V works
at the aggregate with no APC decomposition. The compositional-vs-cascade test
(APC decomposition + parity-progression by union type) stands unaltered and must
precede the next ABM modeling step.
