**3.1 Benzell, Kotlikoff & Ye — the revision-direction inference is backwards.** ~~A *heavier* payroll burden for Mexico under WPP 2024 than under WPP 2017 is what a **downward** revision to fertility and working-age population produces (fewer contributors per beneficiary), not an upward one.~~ **[Replaced 2026-09-23 by Anne, EIC-graphs record `2026-09-23_EIC-rebased-scenarios_Anne-record.md` §4:]** burden direction is not a valid proxy for population-revision direction (it depends on age composition and benefit rules, not on level); read the vintage tables. Successive WPP revisions have moved Mexico's projected population *down*, consistent with the global pattern in F-V&N Table A4. The note's body should state the direction as downward, with the WPP 2017 vs 2024 Mexico 2050 medium-variant figures cited from the two revisions (Debb to pull; I will not assert the numbers from memory). On the concrete ask: WPP 2024 medium keeps Mexico well above observed — the BID2 hard-code carries WPP 2024's Mexico TFR 2050 = 1.70, against EIC 1.23 / registry ≈1.46 / F-V 1.51 today — which is exactly why WPP is the optimistic column by rule. `scenario_implication: neutral` is correct for the note; the body's reasoning is corrected, not overturned. The Kopecky tension (loser vs winner) is not a contradiction once technology adoption is held fixed in one and endogenous in the other; agree with the drafter.

**3.2 Kotschy & Bloom — chronological vs prospective thresholds for the IM-6 age structure. Ruling: chronological stays primary; prospective enters as a reported sensitivity, not an input.** NTA age profiles are chronological by construction and IM-6's demographic block is specified against them; making a prospective old-age threshold (POAT, remaining life expectancy 15) the primary metric would be a documented departure from NTA and would break the module interface. But the point that chronological measures mis-state age-structure effects when e65 moves is valid, and Mexico's e65 gains are moderate enough that the two measures will diverge slowly — which makes the comparison informative rather than noisy. Implementation: the quarterly replicate reports the prospective old-age dependency ratio alongside the chronological TDR as a sensitivity line; no change to what feeds IM-6. Note for Beth: in LAC the binding threshold is neither chronological nor prospective but the *effective* labour-force exit age mediated by informality — that is the measure that would actually move the fiscal result, and it is hers.

**3.3 Bernardino, Franco & Teles Morais — three items.**
- *Emigration-convexity corollary, novel for LAC?* Relevance is now established in our own data (EIC: 1.3 M out 2020–25, 70% male, 20–29 — prime net contributors) and in F-V&N's Colombia point; *novelty* needs the paper's proposition in hand. Defer to file review.
- *θτ as DFD reporting metric?* IM-6 benchmark only. DFD's welfare-metric discipline (CEV, Cath) is not displaced by a rebalancing-tax metric calibrated to the euro area; θτ is a useful comparative benchmark and should be reported as such. Cath co-decides; that is my side of it.

For fast-transition LAC the short-run fertility lever is if anything weaker (momentum), but the claim is not settled in general and must not be cited as if it were. This is the same discipline applied to the WPP rebound: state the assumption behind the result.
