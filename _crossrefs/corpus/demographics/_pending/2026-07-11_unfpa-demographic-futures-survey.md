# UNFPA 2026 Demographic Futures Survey — "Lives, Choices and Futures"

## One-line summary

A 73-country UNFPA survey of 108,926 internet-connected young adults (18–39) finds that
most still want partnership and ~two children, and frames the fertility shortfall as
**constrained reproductive agency** — economic and housing barriers, not diminished desire.

## Core content (2026 report's own figures)

- **Scope.** 108,926 internet-connected young adults aged **18–39** across **73 countries
  and territories**, every geographical region. UNFPA states explicitly the findings are
  **"not nationally representative and should not be interpreted as population-level
  estimates."**
- **Partnership aspiration.** More than **two-thirds** selected an ideal relationship /
  living arrangement that includes marriage or living with a partner.
- **Ideal family size.** **Two children** is the most commonly reported ideal in **five of
  seven** regional groupings; in some regions the average desired number exceeds two.
- **Barriers.** **57%** cited economic and housing constraints as barriers to forming
  partnerships; **72%** cited them as barriers to having children. Economic/housing
  constraints are the single most common obstacle in both cases.
- **Preconditions for parenthood.** Financial security **88%**, stable employment **87%**,
  psychological/emotional readiness **85%**; ~**81%** rated financial security crucial for
  forming partnerships. **80%** named the joy of parenthood as a key factor.
- **Desire persists at older ages.** Among **35–39-year-olds without children**, **79% of
  men** and **72% of women** still want to become parents.
- **Outlook.** **Two-thirds** feel somewhat or very positive about the future despite
  worries about conflict, economic insecurity, and inequality.
- **Framing (verbatim thrust).** "Women are not refusing to have children. They — and men,
  too — face obstacles to having them." UNFPA positions this as a **"baby bust" myth-buster**:
  low fertility as unmet aspiration under constraint, not falling desire.

**Accuracy guard (per build instruction §2).** These are the **2026** Demographic Futures
Survey figures. They are NOT the 2025 State of World Population figures — the widely-quoted
"14-country, 18% expect fewer/more children than they want" statistic belongs to the **2025**
report and is deliberately excluded here.

## The tension flagged for Anne (do NOT resolve here)

UNFPA frames the fertility shortfall as **constraints on reproductive agency** — people
*cannot* have the children they want. The DFD **SDT framing** centers **value/behavior
change** — channel-assisted compression of the extensive margin. These may be
**complementary** (constraints operating *within* a shifted preference/socialization regime)
or **competing** (agency-constraint as an alternative to the SDT account). **This is Anne's
adjudication**, not the note's. Recorded as an open framing question, not settled.

## Source quality

- **Reliability:** `primary` as a UNFPA institutional publication, **but** the sample is an
  **internet-connected convenience sample, explicitly non-representative** — treat every
  percentage as directional aspiration signal, not a population parameter. This caveat is
  load-bearing; do not cite these shares as prevalence.
- **Recency:** report published **7 July 2026** (ahead of World Population Day, 11 July 2026);
  survey fielded 2025–2026.
