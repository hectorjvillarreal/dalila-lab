## 1. Anne — the 65+ gate: ambiguous, and it does not need to be resolved for this note

The check did its job. On 15 Oct 2025 the three counts of people aged 65 and over are:
- EIC: 13.23 M;
- CPV 2020 aged forward: 12.51 M;
- CONAPO: 11.84 M.

CONAPO sits 0.67 M below the aged census and the EIC 0.73 M above it. The executor was right not to choose between them.

**Ruling: v1.0 stays on the EIC base, and the 2025 figures are printed as a range.** Three reasons:
- **The note's claims are insensitive to it.** The CONAPO-65+ sensitivity moves τ by 1.2 points in 2025 and by only 0.2 in 2050. The 2050 older population is aged 40+ today and is driven by survival, not by the 2025 count.
- **Consistency.** The note and the public chart both say "partimos de lo que contó el INEGI". Swapping in a modelled 65+ would break that for no gain in 2035 or 2050.
- **Transparency.** The range is printed rather than hidden (edit 1, §5).

**Hypothesis for the Q4 gate, recorded here.** The EIC excess is concentrated at 65–69 (+8.3 %). A CONAPO undercount would be spread across ages; a bunched excess suggests age reporting shifting across a threshold. Since 2021 the universal non-contributory pension for older adults begins at 65, which makes that age salient in self-report. The test uses the EIC single-year-of-age microdata (release November 2026):
- heaping at 65, with a deficit at 62–64, points to a reporting shift;
- a smooth excess across ages points to CONAPO.

This is logged as a Q4 item and stays out of the note.

## 3. Cath — page 3 and τ: endorsed

- **The minimum.** Central 45.0 in 2030 against 46.4 today: the ratio falls only 1.4 points more, against Q3's 42.0 at ~2038. The before/after is convention-free, and the span sits in Recuadro 2 with its definition and my caveat. Endorsed.
- **OADR beside TDR.** It rises from 14.6 to 33.1 (Central) or 34.2 (INEGI directo) by 2050. The composition point now carries itself. Endorsed.
- **τ = ρ·OADR/e.** ρ = 0.50 is sourced to IM-6. e = 0.6322 comes from ENOE 2026-Q2 microdata, and the coverage parameter is gone. The results are Central 11.6 / 16.2 / 26.2 % and INEGI directo 11.6 / 16.2 / 27.1 % (2025 / 2035 / 2050).
  - Add one sentence, so no reader takes it for a statutory rate (edit 3): the τ is a *stylised benchmark*, the contribution on all workers' earnings that would fund a replacement rate of one-half in a pay-as-you-go system.
  - **The strongest fiscal line in the note:** the path to 2035 is identical across scenarios. Nothing that happens to fertility changes what pensions cost over the next decade. Keep it on page 3, first after the minimum.
- **Growth.** 15–64 starts falling in 2036 in all four scenarios. Endorsed.
