## 2. The regional rail

The rule of 85 — long-run population ≈ 85 × annual births, equivalently a CBR below
1000/85 = **11.76 per thousand** meaning births are already insufficient to hold
population constant — is applied to the region as a whole, which the cornerstone paper
does not do:

- LAC births peaked in **1990 at 12.0 million**, implying a long-run 1.02 billion.
- They are now around **8 million**, implying **680 million** — and at LAC's actual
  e₀ of ~76 rather than 85, **608 million**, some **8% below today's 668 million**.
- On that basis the deck **rejects ECLAC's projected regional peak of 730 million in
  2053**.

Supporting regional accounting: LAC ran a CBR near 42 per thousand and a TFR near 6 in
the early 1950s; life expectancy went from 52 in the late 1950s to 71 by the late
1990s and is above 75 today; natural increase peaked at **2.8% a year in the early
1960s**, the fastest any large region has sustained; urbanisation went from 41% (1950)
to 86%. LAC rose from 2.0% of world population in 1750 to **8.6% in 2000**, and has
since fallen to **8.1% (668 M of 8,231 M in 2025)** — the first decline on record.

## 4. Re-anchor trigger check (Q3 §2) — **does not fire**

Q3 armed the trigger: *if INEGI publishes a TGF ≤ 1.50 for vintage 2024 or 2025, the
Central anchor goes to Anne.* Her pre-committed frame: a published TGF in [1.45, 1.50]
re-pins Central at the published value; below 1.45, open a fifth scenario row and keep
Central at 1.50 as the upper bracket.

The deck reports **Mexico 1.51 (2025)**. It misses on three legs:

1. **Threshold.** 1.51 > 1.50 — one hundredth above a stated threshold.
2. **Source.** It is Fernández-Villaverde's own tabulation, `secondary` under the
   corpus reliability rule and absent from the top of the Mexico source hierarchy.
   The trigger keys on an **INEGI publication**; Anne's sourcing rule 2 is explicit
   that secondary aggregators corroborate but are not the citation.
3. **It is not a registry figure at all.** The cornerstone paper's footnote 3 states
   that **only about 72% of a year's births in Mexico are registered in the year they
   occur**, and that *"the 2025 figure extrapolates the births registered within 2025
   to an expected final tally."* It is a secondary **extrapolation** — one step further
   from an INEGI publication than a registry value.

**The recorded tension, now explained.** The deck's 1.51 (2025) sits *above* Q3's
registry-implied ≈ 1.46 (2024). Leg 3 accounts for it: the two are not on comparable
bases — Q3 scaled the ENADID 2023 survey anchor by the ENR fertile-age rate decline,
while the deck extrapolates within-year registrations under a 72% completeness rate —
and **the deck's figure is the weaker of the two**. This is an argument for waiting on
INEGI, not for moving now, and it sharpens Q4 follow-ups 5 (watch for a 2024/2025 TGF)
and 7 (the occurred-vs-registered wedge).

Anne's ruling on the alternative question — whether a credible secondary reading at
1.51 is itself grounds to re-examine Central ahead of INEGI — is **no**. The
pre-committed frame keys on a published TGF precisely so Central is not chased around
point estimates. Noted for the record: **Q3's own registry-implied 1.46 already sits
inside the [1.45, 1.50] re-pin band**, so if INEGI publishes in that neighbourhood,
Central re-pins at the published value with no further deliberation.
