# Fertility reversibility — does constraint-relief close the desired-achieved gap?

## Origin

Surfaced by the UNFPA Demographic Futures Survey note (2026-07-11) and
adjudicated in Anne's endorsement record of the same date; opened as an
endorsed watch item on execution of that record (2026-08-03). The UNFPA
framing (constrained reproductive agency) and the DFD SDT framing
(value/behavior regime change) were ruled **complementary, at different
levels — not competing**. What remains open is not the framing debate but
its policy corollary.

## The thread

If the unmet-desire, policy-tractable reading of low fertility has weight,
the DFD fast-transition Central path carries a latent upside risk it does
not currently represent (pro-natal / housing / childcare constraint-relief
producing recovery); the European SDT precedent — no reversal on economic
improvement — is the null.

**Scope exclusion:** this item is NOT the agency-constraint-vs-SDT framing
debate, which is settled as complementary (see the endorsement guardrail in
the surfacing entry: aspiration ≠ realized behavior; persistent two-child
ideals do not refute the SDT/cohort account).

## Modeling implication

A demonstrated constraint-relief → fertility-recuperation channel would
justify an explicit policy-response scenario alongside the Central
(TFR = 1.50) path in the MEX quarterly replicate scenario family, and would
qualify the permanent-loss reading of the coupling-rate decline in the
standing analytical considerations (§1, DFD_TFR_forecast_instructions).
Absent that evidence, the sticky-regime null stands and no scenario is
added.

## Open questions

- Is there credible quasi-experimental evidence anywhere that
  constraint-relief policy (housing, childcare, transfers) closes the
  desired-achieved fertility gap, rather than shifting timing?
- Does any middle- or upper-middle-income case — the relevant comparison
  class for Mexico — show period-fertility recuperation attributable to
  such policy?

## Status

Watch item, not active commitment. Cadence: low.

## Triggers for promotion

1. Credible quasi-experimental evidence that constraint-relief policy
   closes the desired-achieved gap in a middle- or upper-middle-income
   setting.
2. A Mexican policy shift of sufficient scale plus observed
   period-fertility recuperation.
