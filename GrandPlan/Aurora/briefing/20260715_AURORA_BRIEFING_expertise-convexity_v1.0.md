# Briefing — Expertise convexity as a cohort-structured inequality mechanism

**Ask.** Anne's judgment on three things: (1) whether this duplicates the

## The empirical finding

Source: Ethan Mollick, "The twilight of the chatbots" (One Useful Thing,
2026-06-30), reporting an Anthropic study of Claude Code users.

Success in using AI agents was predicted **not by profession but by domain
expertise**. Software engineers showed a success rate similar to other
professions on coding tasks; a lawyer working in a familiar domain performed
comparably to an engineer working in an unfamiliar one. The second half of the
finding is the operative one: **the more domain experience a user had, the more
useful output they extracted per prompt**.

## Why this is not the generic "AI increases inequality" claim

Two features make it specific enough to be worth modelling.

**1. The skill variable is tacit expertise, not education or credential.**
This is skill-biased technical change with the skill term replaced. Not years
of schooling, not occupational category — accumulated domain-specific tacit
knowledge. Hayek's particular knowledge, priced. This connects directly to the
Kiesling frame already in the Aurora corpus: AI raises the return to judgment,
tacit knowledge, and institutional understanding.

**2. The return appears convex, not merely positive.**
If more expertise yields more output *per prompt*, expertise functions as a
**multiplier on agent output** rather than an additive advantage. The
implication: as capability improves, the expert–novice gap *widens*. This
supplies a mechanism and a distributional shape for the Amodei
"hypergrowth / hyper-inequality" claim, which asserts the outcome without
specifying the channel. Displacement is not uniform — it falls on the
non-expert while amplifying the expert.

## The DFD-native hook: this is a cohort object, not a cross-section

The mechanism becomes an OLG object when combined with the apprenticeship
problem (raised by Kiesling; echoed in a different idiom by Conti, and flagged
separately to Cath).

If expertise is the multiplier, and expertise historically accumulated through
precisely the routine tasks agents now absorb, then the **human capital
accumulation function changes differentially by cohort**:

- Cohorts that accumulated tacit expertise **pre-AI** hold the multiplier and
  capture rents.
- Cohorts entering **now** face an automated first rung and are displaced
  rather than amplified.

This is not visible in a cross-sectional Gini on current income. It is a
cohort-specific return to experience — which is exactly what the DFD machinery
represents and standard inequality measurement does not.

**The compounding with demographic transition (the reason this is DFD's and not
labour economics').** An aging society contains proportionally *more* of the
cohorts holding the multiplier and *fewer* of those harmed. The political
economy therefore locks the arrangement in: the beneficiary cohorts are also
the median voter. Two adverse dynamics compounding — but domestic, not
geopolitical.

## The measurement problem (likely the most immediately useful part)

If the relevant variable is **tacit domain expertise**, standard instruments
may miss the axis entirely. Education, occupation, and sector do not observe
it. ENIGH does not observe it.

The predicted signature is therefore **rising within-occupation,
within-education dispersion driven by an unmeasured variable**. If that is what
is happening, conventional decompositions will attribute the variance to
residuals and the mechanism will be invisible in the data we have.

This is a measurement problem before it is a modelling problem — Anne's seat.
Open question: is there any instrument, in LAC data or elsewhere, that proxies
accumulated tacit expertise independent of tenure and credential?

## Caveats — material, and should travel with the finding

- **Self-interested measurement.** The Claude Code study is Anthropic's own,
  about its own product. Directionally plausible and consistent with the
  Kiesling prior, but provenance is not neutral and should not be laundered by
  citation.
- **Single study, single context.** US, largely technical/professional users.
  Transfer to LAC labour markets — with high informality, thinner professional
  strata, different credential signalling — is unestablished.
- **"Convexity" is an inference**, not a measured functional form. The study
  reports more output per prompt with more expertise; the shape of that
  relationship is not estimated.
- Mollick's own jagged-frontier caveat applies to all capability claims in the
  source.
