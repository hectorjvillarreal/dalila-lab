## 1. Two corrections to my own work

**The CVR threshold was mis-specified — mine.** I booked it as "MY2027 software
prohibition due 2026-09-30." §1's premise check is right: the prohibition is keyed to
**model year 2027** under §791.308, not to a calendar date, and no primary source names
09-30. The date came from a law-firm snippet in the pass-002 flags brief, which I hardened
into a dated threshold without primary verification.

> **Discipline note, for the scaffold:** a threshold inherits the confidence of the source
> that dated it. A `reported` date must not become a booked threshold. This is the
> `confidence` field's logic applied one level up, to thresholds rather than slots — and
> it is the second time a secondary source has propagated into the board's structure
> (cf. the Leapmotor "narrow lineup" error, 2026-08-16).

**The VW watch-item correction was also wrong — also mine.** On 2026-09-13 I called the
watch item *"whether a European plant is actually sold to a Chinese rival"* mis-worded,
because Osnabrück went to Aurelius with Rafael as anchor customer. §5 records a BYD adviser
stating BYD wants to buy and fully own a European plant, focusing on Spain and France.
The wording was right; the event had not happened yet. **The watch item stands unchanged** —
and my correction to it is withdrawn.

## 2. TH-001 / TH-002 — the three regimes resolve

In September I set out three possible regimes for entry 001's 2b bet (acquired legitimacy).
§1 settles which obtains.

| Regime | Status |
|---|---|
| Executive discretion (09-11 statement) | **Did not materialise.** Autos absent from both the White House fact sheet and the PRC readout. |
| Governance test (Connected Vehicle Rule) | **Unchanged.** No Federal Register action on 15 CFR 791; rule operative. |
| Statutory bright line (S.4429, >15% equity) | **Advanced.** Reported 2026-09-22, Cal. No. 671, equity bar intact. |

**Verdict: the worst of the three for acquired legitimacy.** The discretionary opening
closed; the bright-line threat advanced. Disentanglement — what saved Volvo under the CVR —
is **no defence against an ownership test**, because no amount of governance separation
changes a cap table.

**Both thresholds remain open**, but their decisive instrument has changed: they now rest on
S.4429's floor progress rather than on CVR adjudication. The instrument that would decide
them has moved but not passed. *Re-aim both when the entry is next touched.*

## 3. New finding — acquired legitimacy is a contagion vector

§1 records Mercedes' two Chinese holders at **19.67%**, against S.4429's >15% bar. A
threshold on *capital* rather than on *firm nationality* catches a German incumbent.

This inverts entry 001's framing. Acquired legitimacy was read as a weak asset for the
**acquirer** — conferred, revocable, per-marque. It is also a **liability for the acquired**:
a minority stake taken to buy access can cost the target its own. Geely's holding in
Mercedes is the live case.

**Status: flagged, not filed.** The 19.67% figure is secondary and analyses the *introduced*
rather than the *reported* text. Verification against the reported Sec. 3(6) definition,
set against actual Mercedes shareholdings, is required before this enters `findings.md`.
A finding that inverts an entry's framing may not rest on a secondary reading of a
superseded draft.

## 4. Entry 008 — Leapmotor, specification

§2 supplies enough to write it. Three facts, **in this order of weight**:

1. **Zaragoza.** B10 in production at Stellantis's Spanish plant, with Stellantis's CEO
   stating Leapmotor and Dongfeng models will fill idle European capacity. *This is the
   channel inversion made physical — and it is precisely what Blume floated as VW's
   alternative to closure (§5). Stellantis executed what VW could only propose.*
2. **Margin compression.** H1 gross margin 11.7% against 14.1% a year earlier (Q1 9.4%,
   Q2 12.6%); full-year profit target cut from CNY 5B to ~3B. The profitable-diffusion
   story holds directionally and is weakening. **The entry leads on this, not on the
   growth number** — Jan–Sep 666,539 (+68.52%) against a 1M target is the headline and
   the least informative fact available.
3. **Dual backing** — Stellantis 18.99%, FAW ~5%, unchanged. No other player on the board
   straddles a Western incumbent and a Chinese state giant, at a moment when the board's
   own geopolitical finding is that blocs are hardening.

**Archetype: expect a mixture, not a corner.** Full-domain in-house R&D and cost
compression say diffusion integrator; the dual backing and rapid span-building say
otherwise. If it lands as a mixture, **pole 1 remains single-exemplar and the
pair-completion defect is unrepaired** — which must be stated in the entry, not left
implicit. Settled when the entry is written.

## 5. BYD — the reading corrected

§3's sequence: Q1 net profit CNY 4.09B (−55.38%); Q2 derived ~8.24B; H1 12.33B (−20.54%)
on revenue −7.13%; gross margin 18.85%. Q3 unpublished.

The entry's stress reading cites **Q1 −55% — the worst quarter in the sequence, used as the
trend.** Corrected reading: profit fell hard in Q1, roughly doubled sequentially in Q2, and
the half closed 20.5% down on revenue down 7.1%. **Margin compression is real; *collapse* is
not the right word.**

Excluded from the reading: the Q2 "+30% YoY" is snippet-only, and the September domestic
figure is contested at the sign level (−12.97% vs +12.8%). Neither carries weight.

## 6. `002 cost_position` — a finding, not a gap

Third consecutive pass with no company-disclosed unit cost (§6). The only candidates are an
analyst derivation (~¥105,000/vehicle, method unstated, internally inconsistent) and an
unsourced cell cost. Tesla defines the metric and prints no figure.

**The decisive variable for BYD's bet is one nobody discloses — so the bet is not testable
as written.** This is a defect in the entry, not a shortfall in the feed, and three passes
is enough evidence to call it.

**Required: re-specify the decisive slot to something observable.** Candidate: gross margin
at stated segment price points — disclosed, and bearing on the same claim (whether cost
dominance is being sustained). *Elle's call; not yet made.*

> **For the scaffold:** a bet whose decisive slot names an undisclosed variable is
> unfalsifiable in practice. Slots are derived from bets — but the derivation must also ask
> whether the variable is *observable*. Add to `discipline.md` when the next repair runs.

## 7. Honda — correction stands, with a sharper claim available

§4: Q2 FY3/27 unpublished (early November). Q1 FY3/27 operating profit ¥530.8B (+117.4%);
full-year guidance raised ¥500B → ¥650B. My "bad position" judgment rested on the FY3/26
write-down year and **was wrong as a group-level claim.**

But §4 also gives Honda China September at 25,564 (−53.1%), with GAC Honda −86%.

**The sharper claim: Honda is recovering at group level while its China position collapses —
the Hyundai pattern (007), not the VW pattern (005).** Losing China is survivable when the
profit pool is elsewhere. *Mazda, by contrast, holds: FY3/26 operating margin ~1.0%, own
BEV pushed 2027→2029, no comparable cushion.* The original grouping of Honda with Mazda was
the error.

## 8. Entry 003 — the decisive watch item is answering

§7 records HIMA at 367,229 Jan–Sep — a **fourth straight YoY decline** against the entry's
"push to 1M+" — and Seres taking over Aito's brand functions on 09-30.

Entry 003's decisive watch item asks whether the stack supplier commoditises its partners
into assemblers. **A partner reclaiming brand control is the first real evidence, and it
points the other way.** One partner, one action: direction recorded, not concluded.

*Note the cross-reference: this is the same variable the Suzuki and Renault generator runs
landed on — stack rentability. If partners can reclaim control, the frontier layer is
rentable on survivable terms, and the small-player question resolves more favourably than
the board assumed.*
