# Entry 004 — Toyota  ·  *multi-path hedger (4a, high) + tempo skeptic (4b, high)*

*Pole 4 split 2026-07-24; this entry holds both resulting axes. Under the old bundled
pole Toyota and Hyundai were indistinguishable — they are now one cell apart on the 4a×4b
grid (`../archetypes.md#the-4a--4b-grid-2026-07-24`).*

**Structure:** world's largest automaker (~10M+/yr), hybrid-first, multi-pathway (HEV/PHEV/BEV/hydrogen). **CEO Kenta Kon** (ex-CFO, appointed early 2026); chairman Akio Toyoda (publicly holds BEVs cap near ~30% share). **Koji Sato** — CEO 2023–26, now on the board as Toyota's **first chief industry officer** and **head of JAMA**. Owns Daihatsu and Lexus; minority stakes in Subaru, Mazda, Suzuki.

**The bet (falsifiable) — 4b:** the BEV transition is slower, messier, and more infrastructure-bound than China prices; a hybrid-heavy hedge prints profit through the transition while keeping optionality. *(Counter: everyone running the diffusion thesis.)*
**Reading:** the wager has *already paid once* — in the windows where Western BEV growth stalled, the hybrid lineup buffered profit while pure-BEV players bled. Open question: vindication or merely *delay*? If diffusion drives BEV cost decisively below parity, the hedge becomes the reason Toyota is behind on BEV scale, software, and in China.
**Why 4a and 4b are held separately:** Toyota is high on both, which is why the original pole fused them. Hyundai (007) is high on 4a and low on 4b — the case that proved them separable. The distinction is not cosmetic: **the hedge is a capability and survives being wrong; the skepticism is a belief and does not.** Great Wall ran 4b-high with a weak 4a in a fast-transition market and was eliminated.

**Coordination capacity (2026-07-20) — cross-level, not an archetype.** Sato is pushing industry-wide standardisation across Japanese automakers: wiring harnesses first (~70,000 supplier variants today, which obstructs automation), then steel grades and resin specs — a claimed ten-fold productivity gain, with savings redeployed into ADAS, faster-charging batteries and software. Six further cooperation areas: logistics, raw-materials access, circular recycling, talent, AV infrastructure, and simplifying Japan's auto taxes. Nissan's Ivan Espinosa backs a certified "JAMA standard" for shared commodities. **This is diffusion logic — commonality, scale, cost compression — adopted defensively at the *system* level.** A firm that moves its ex-CEO into an industry-coordination role is signalling where it thinks the decisive game is played.
*Filed as a cross-level variable, not pole 4c (`../archetypes.md#cross-level-variables--not-archetypes`).* The act is firm-level; its **availability is environmentally gated** — Japan has an association tradition, Germany has co-determination and got paralysis instead. Entering it as a firm archetype would attribute an institutional endowment to a corporate strategy.
**Does not falsify the tempo bet — isolates it.** "The industry will not survive without cooperation" concerns *competitive threat*; the hedge concerns *transition tempo*. Separate axes; both can hold. **This is what made the pole split tractable, and it is why the third component landed at a different level rather than as a third pole.**
**The clock (falsifiable):** ~1–2 years to settle specs, rollout only with the next vehicle generation → ~5–8 years to effect, against a Chinese iteration cadence in months. **Coordination is slow by construction — consent is the price.** Same structure as VW's institutional latency, one level up. *Threshold: are harness specs actually agreed by end-2027?* (Booked as TH-003 in `../thresholds.yaml`.)

**Watch items:** *(Gina, light)* Japan's hydrogen-as-industrial-policy and China exposure · solid-state credibility (timeline slipped 2020→23→26→**2027-28**) · BEV ramp pace vs the diffusion cost curve · China share erosion.
