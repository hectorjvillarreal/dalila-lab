# Entry 005 — Volkswagen Group (complete)  ·  *declining span, institutionally constrained (relabel tested 2026-07-09, did not fire)*

**Structure:** the Western mirror of Geely — span conglomerate from mass (VW, Škoda, Cupra) through premium (Audi) to halo (Porsche, Lamborghini, Bentley), plus Traton (MAN/Scania), PowerCo, Cariad (software). CEO Oliver Blume.
**The bet (and its misfire):** went all-in early on BEV (MEB, ID. family, in-house software) — and got punished. Cariad stumbled, the ID. line underwhelmed, and the China profit engine is collapsing.
**Western-mirror contrast (sharpened 2026-06-26):** same archetype as Geely, run in opposite directions — **Geely consolidates offensively** (folding Zeekr/Lynk into one group, buying legitimacy up/out); **VW may now disaggregate defensively** — the reported plan to spin off the *core VW brand* and the *components business* into separate listable entities is the span conglomerate beginning to take itself apart. Not "declining span" but possible *un-conglomeration*. Offensive consolidation vs defensive disaggregation.
**The June 26 escalation (reported):** Manager Magazin reported up to **100,000 job cuts** (~15% of ~657k staff — double the prior 50k), closure of **four German plants** (VW Hanover, Zwickau, Emden; Audi Neckarsulm), a 15% investment cut, and a spinoff of the core VW brand + components into separate listable entities. Zwickau is the all-EV conversion plant idled on weak demand — the early-BEV bet's most visible asset first on the list. VW's own line: the make-in-Germany-export-to-world model "no longer works" across all brands — *the diffusion thesis stated by its victim.*

**Threshold event resolved — July 9 supervisory board. Verdict: relabel does NOT fire.** Labour representatives **blocked** the plan. VW's post-meeting statement mentioned neither job cuts nor plant closures; it announced instead further capacity reduction and a **gradual halving of the ~150-line model lineup**. No decision on the spinoff. July 13: Blume's internal memo confirms the 100k figure for the first time (another 50k on top of 50k agreed), pegged to a **20% cost disadvantage vs comparable rivals**; says he still cannot confirm competitive uses for Emden/Hanover/Zwickau/Neckarsulm in the 2030s but prefers "intelligent solutions" to closure. Conciliatory tone; framed as the start of a negotiation, not a decision.

**The finding — restructuring took the shape the institution permitted, not the shape the strategy demanded.** Management cannot close plants (co-determination: labour holds half the supervisory-board seats; Lower Saxony's 20% voting stake + the VW Law limit closure authority) and cannot impose the cuts. What it *can* do without labour's signature is halve the model range — so that is what emerged. **Halving the lineup *is* span reduction**: the declining-span label is confirmed, but the span is declining along the *axis of least institutional resistance*, not by strategic design. VW isn't choosing to shed span; span is its only degree of freedom. Gina's Applebaum lens in corporate form: the institution can absorb and slow the shock, but cannot close a 20% cost gap.

**Blume's named alternatives to closure (both Gina items):** (a) **producing Chinese-market VW models in Europe** — German plants saved by assembling China-developed cars; the XPeng reversal carried to its endpoint; (b) **defence work** — Osnabrück reportedly linked to Iron Dome component production with Rafael; auto capacity absorbed by European rearmament.
**The flagship fact — the XPeng reversal (deepened):** VW now builds China models on **XPeng's E/E architecture** (e.g. ID. UNYX 08) for platform-driven scale. The incumbent licensing architecture *from* the Chinese challenger it's losing to = the frontier hierarchy inverted in a contract.

Can a politically-embedded champion break its own social contract fast enough to survive the wave?
