## 2a · Segment span — the axis Geely won on, and had never wagered on

**The finding the split produced.** The entry as written carried **no falsifiable claim on span at all** — the bet was stated entirely on legitimacy. H1-2026 then delivered #1 in China *on span*, while the legitimacy half was being denied in Washington. **A pole split can reveal that the board was holding an unclaimed axis:** the win was real, and there was no wager on it to vindicate or kill. Under the bundled pole this was invisible, because the two properties shared one bet.

**The 2a bet (written 2026-07-24; previously absent, falsifiable):** breadth across price bands buys resilience when a market contracts — a player present in every band loses less than one concentrated in any single band. *Supporting evidence:* China domestic retail H1-2026 down 20.2%; Geely −16.7% (1.021M) against BYD −38.5% (990,900), with the Galaxy mass NEV line named as the resilience source.

**Already contested by the same dataset.** **Leapmotor** was the only top-ten *grower* in H1-2026 (~90k monthly retail in June, +372.6% exports) running a **narrow** lineup concentrated in the ¥60,000–200,000 band amid consumption downgrading. So **span won the level; narrowness won the rate.** *Kill condition:* if narrow-band players keep outgrowing wide-span ones through the elimination cycle, 2a describes an **incumbency advantage in decline** rather than a strategy. This is the entry's most exposed claim and it is exposed on the axis it just won.

## 2b · Acquired legitimacy — conferred, not owned

**Distinctive asset (re-specified 2026-07-14):** *not an owned stock — a revocable, per-marque, per-regulator license.* **Volvo authorized (May 2026); Polestar denied (June 25) — same parent.** The US Connected Vehicle Rule keys on governance, software sourcing and data infrastructure, *not* assembly address (Polestar 3 is built in South Carolina and banned anyway). Volvo: separately listed, European data infrastructure, own governance → in. Polestar: entangled with Geely's structure, shares platforms/software with Geely brands, Geely servers in China → out. Lotus, smart: untested.
**The bet (falsifiable) — and the tension the rule exposes:** stated as "owned global legitimacy **+** architectural integration beats the diffusion machine in the West." **The conjunction is self-undermining.** Polestar was disqualified *by the very integration that makes the diffusion machine work* (SEA platform/software sharing); Volvo qualified *by standing apart*. Integration is the cost weapon and it destroys legitimacy value; legitimacy demands disentanglement and forfeits integration. Geely cannot hold both in the same marque. *(Counter: BYD — holds no acquired legitimacy **by choice**, betting cost dominance makes it unnecessary.)*
**Domain note:** 2b applies to Geely because its nationality gates its market access. It is **not applicable** to VW (native access) and **not applicable** to Hyundai (native, not acquired) — which is why the Geely↔VW mirror is a contrast on 2a and a non-comparison here (`../005-volkswagen.md`).
**2b is currently single-exemplar**, and therefore carries exactly the defect just repaired. Priority second exemplar: **SAIC/MG** — a British marque as the European access vehicle, same pole, different player.
