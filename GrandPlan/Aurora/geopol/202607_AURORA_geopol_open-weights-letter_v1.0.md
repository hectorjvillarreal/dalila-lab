# Open Weights and American AI Leadership — Corpus Entry

## Register note

**Position document — collective.** This is lobbying with a legible book (the
caveat already logged in the watch-item §7 stands): NVIDIA sells more silicon
under diffusion; the open-weight houses argue their own model is the public
good. Its corpus value is as a **coalition signal** — which firms publicly
commit to the diffusion pole, and which abstain.

## What the letter argues

Dated 2026-07-24. Core analogy: open-source software in the 1980s–90s —
"a shared foundation of knowledge" now underlying the internet, large firms,
and US military/federal missions. The claims, in order:

1. **Leadership = ecosystem, not model.** "Our AI leadership will be judged
   not by one frontier AI model, but by whether the United States builds a
   strong, open ecosystem that diffuses into every sector."
2. **Access economics.** Open weights let organizations "match the right model
   to the right job at the right cost," reserving frontier-scale capability
   for genuine frontier problems — the discipline that makes AI economically
   sustainable "as its use scales into the billions of everyday tasks."
3. **Competition** across model developers, cloud, chips, applications,
   services.
4. **Customer control.** Organizations "will not become locked into a single
   provider or lose the knowledge and capabilities they build over time";
   open weights let them "own that value through self-improving models,
   specialized capabilities, and accumulated knowledge."
5. **Risk acknowledged, prohibition rejected.** Released weights are beyond
   the developer's control; but defenders need comparable capability, and
   openness enables inspection, red-teaming, and "protections tied to real
   and demonstrated harms rather than assuming that closed systems are safer
   by default."
6. **Policy asks:** compute access for startups/researchers; shared training
   assets (datasets, tools, eval frameworks); "keeping the frontier plural by
   avoiding premature restrictions on open models"; strong application layers
   for "sovereign use of AI across the economy."
7. **Distillation defense.** Distillation is "a widely used technique for
   model improvement, evaluation, and validation," not misappropriation;
   unlawful extraction should be handled by "targeted legal and commercial
   frameworks rather than sweeping restrictions."

## Findings

1. **The watch-item §7 configuration, now documented from the primary.** The
   US runs capability-as-chokepoint (state: the June export-control order, the
   June 2 EO gating framework) and capability-as-diffusion (this letter)
   simultaneously. The letter is the diffusion pole's formal brief, filed
   twenty-four days after the chokepoint instrument was exercised.
2. **The Nadella tension, sharpened.** Point 4 is the Reverse Information
   Paradox remedy argued nearly verbatim — own your learning loop, don't leak
   accumulated knowledge to a provider — but from the *open-weight* side,
   with Microsoft as signatory. Nadella's essay sells the trust boundary as a
   managed service inside Microsoft's cloud; Microsoft's signature here backs
   weights the customer runs anywhere. Consistent as a hedge across both
   poles; the seam logged in the Nadella entry ("decentralisation within
   centralised infrastructure") now has Microsoft on both sides of it.
3. **Karp convergence extends.** Palantir signs. The "own the means of
   production" line quoted in the Nadella entry now has a policy-document
   expression.
4. **Timing against Beijing.** The letter lands as MOFCOM consults on
   restricting overseas access to Chinese open weights (watch-item v1.1 §6).
   If both materialize, the poles swap: US industry lobbying to keep weights
   open while China closes them — inverting the mid-2026 board in which
   Chinese open weights are the diffusion layer.
